from sklearn.compose import ColumnTransformer
from sklearn.pipeline import Pipeline
from sklearn.preprocessing import OneHotEncoder, StandardScaler
from sklearn.impute import SimpleImputer

categorical_pipeline = Pipeline([
    ("imputer", SimpleImputer(strategy="most_frequent")),
    ("encoder", OneHotEncoder(handle_unknown="ignore"))
])

numerical_pipeline = Pipeline([
    ("imputer", SimpleImputer(strategy="median")),
    ("scaler", StandardScaler())
])

preprocessor = ColumnTransformer([
    ("categorical", categorical_pipeline, categorical_cols),
    ("numerical", numerical_pipeline, numerical_cols)
])

from sklearn.pipeline import Pipeline
from sklearn.base import clone
from sklearn.tree import DecisionTreeClassifier
from sklearn.ensemble import GradientBoostingClassifier
from sklearn.metrics import accuracy_score, roc_auc_score

prep = rf_model.named_steps["preprocessor"]

extra = {
    "Decision Tree": DecisionTreeClassifier(max_depth=6, class_weight="balanced", random_state=42),
    "Gradient Boosting": GradientBoostingClassifier(n_estimators=200, learning_rate=0.05, max_depth=3, random_state=42),
}

extra_proba = {}
rows = []
for name, clf in extra.items():
    m = Pipeline([("preprocessor", clone(prep)), ("model", clf)])
    m.fit(X_train, y_train)
    pred = m.predict(X_test)
    proba = m.predict_proba(X_test)[:, 1]
    extra_proba[name] = proba
    rows.append({"Model": name,
                 "Accuracy": accuracy_score(y_test, pred),
                 "Precision": precision_score(y_test, pred, pos_label="yes"),
                 "Recall": recall_score(y_test, pred, pos_label="yes"),
                 "F1-score": f1_score(y_test, pred, pos_label="yes"),
                 "ROC-AUC": roc_auc_score(y_test, proba)})

results_all = pd.concat([results, pd.DataFrame(rows)], ignore_index=True)

Recommendations for the Marketing Team¶

  1. Use cellular contact instead of telephone. Cellular calls converted at 14.7% (20,908 calls) against 5.3% for telephone (12,042 calls), about 2.8 times higher. Part of this gap may reflect when each channel was used, so I recommend testing before moving the whole budget.

  2. Prioritise customers with a previous successful campaign. 64.9% of customers whose previous outcome was "success" subscribed, against 8.8% for those with no previous campaign. The group is small (1,105 customers), so it is a high-value follow-up list rather than a volume source.

  3. Target students and retired customers. Students subscribed at 30.5% (711 customers) and retired customers at 25.5% (1,366), against 11.3% overall. Blue-collar workers had the lowest rate (6.9%) but are the largest group after admin (7,441 calls), so they use up a lot of call time for few subscriptions.

  4. Rethink the timing of campaign effort. May had the most calls (11,011, about a third of all calls) and the lowest rate (6.3%). December, March, September and October converted at 43% to 50%, but each had under 600 calls, so those rates may reflect which customers were called rather than the month itself. A sensible step is to test a shift of some calls into these months and measure the result.

  5. Rank customers with the model. Gradient Boosting at a threshold of about 0.2 found 59% of subscribers with 48% of calls successful, about 4 times the 11.3% random baseline. Calling the highest-scoring customers first improves results for the same call budget.

  6. Avoid repeated calls to the same customer. In the model, a higher number of contacts in the current campaign slightly lowered the chance of subscribing.

Caveat: These are associations in historical data, not proven causes, and the economic indicators (euribor 3m, employment variation) were also among the strongest predictors, so results depend partly on market conditions at the time of the call.
